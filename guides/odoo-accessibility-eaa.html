European Accessibility Act: a checklist for Odoo shops

November 2026 · Odoo 18, 19 and 20 · This guide is not legal advice.

Since 28 June 2025, the European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) applies to e-commerce services offered to consumers in the European Union. If you sell online with Odoo, your shop is probably concerned.

Who is concerned

  • Businesses that sell products or services online to consumers in the EU.
  • Microenterprises (fewer than 10 people and an annual turnover or balance sheet of at most 2 million euros) are exempted for the services they provide.
  • Each country enforces the act through its own authorities, which can ask for corrections and impose penalties.

Which standard to follow

The harmonised European standard EN 301 549 refers to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 level AA. WCAG 2.2 level AA includes all of them and adds a few criteria. In France, the reference is the RGAA, also based on WCAG. You are also expected to publish an accessibility statement describing how your service meets the requirements.

What to check on an Odoo website

Some problems come from the pages provided by Odoo, most from the content you add yourself.

CheckWhere it usually fails in Odoo
Images have a text alternativeImages inserted with the website editor get an empty description by default
Everything works with the keyboard, and the focus is visibleShop filters and some menus show no focus indicator
Form fields have labels and autofillCheckout and contact forms have no autofill tokens
Links and buttons have a meaningful nameIcon-only buttons, "click here" links in your texts
Text contrast is at least 4.5:1Light grey texts, colours chosen for your theme
Headings follow a logical orderHeading levels chosen for their size in the editor

Free tools help: the axe DevTools browser extension, the accessibility score of Lighthouse, and simply navigating your checkout with the Tab key only.

Why an overlay is not enough

Accessibility toolbars ("overlays") add buttons on top of the page, such as bigger text or high contrast. They do not change the code that screen readers read, so the errors stay. The fixes have to be made in the pages themselves.
